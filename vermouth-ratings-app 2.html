Key vermouth botanicals

The herbs, roots and seeds you're most likely to meet. Wormwood is the defining one: in the EU a drink can only be called vermouth if its flavour comes from wormwood (Artemisia).

WormwoodBitter, resinous and sage-like. The backbone of every vermouth, and where the name comes from (German Wermut).
GentianDeep, earthy, lingering bitterness. Drives the finish, especially in rossos.
CinchonaQuinine bark. A clean, dry, tonic-water bitterness.
AngelicaMusky, earthy and celery-like. Adds depth and helps tie other botanicals together.
Orris rootPowdery, violet-like perfume. Softens edges and lengthens aroma.
CalamusBitter with warm ginger and cinnamon tones.
Rhubarb rootEarthy, slightly smoky, tart bitterness.
SageSavoury and slightly camphor-like. Very common in dry and bianco styles.
Thyme and marjoramWarm Mediterranean herb notes, savoury rather than sweet.
HyssopMinty, medicinal and faintly floral.
Yarrow and centauryTraditional bittering herbs, grassy and drying.
Chamomile and elderflowerHoneyed apple and soft floral notes, typical of bianco and Chambéry styles.
Coriander seedCitrusy and lightly spicy. Bridges the herbs and the orange peel.
Cardamom, clove, cinnamon, nutmegWarm baking spice, strongest in rosso.
SaffronHoneyed, hay-like and slightly metallic. Gives some vermouths their golden colour.
